The Role Was Never the Code

By Win · First posted on LinkedIn, May 2026

Software engineering has always been the same job.

We learn a language, a framework, a stack — and use them to build something a person will use. The code was the medium. The user was the point.

1960s
AssemblyThe role was to deliver working software.
1980s
CThe role was to deliver working software.
1990s
Java and high-level OOPThe role was to deliver working software.
2010s
Cloud services and packagesThe role was to deliver working software.
Today
Gen AIWe specify behavior, generate the implementation, review, refine, ship. The role is to deliver working software.

The role has been constant for seventy years. The tools have changed completely.

What the role has always required:

  • 01Understanding what is worth building
  • 02Specifying behavior precisely
  • 03 · BEING ABSTRACTEDProducing the implementation
  • 04Verifying that it works
  • 05Maintaining it as the world changes
  • 06Owning the outcome for the user

One of six is shifting to Gen AI. The other five remain on the engineer.

Producing the implementation is one of six. It is the one being abstracted right now. The other five remain entirely on the engineer. That is where engineering judgment lives.

One more thing has been constant alongside the role: the obligation to learn.

Today, the investment has two halves, and both matter.

The first is learning to use Gen AI effectively. Not autocomplete. Specifying behavior, structuring context, evaluating output, integrating generated code, maintaining quality at speed — these are real disciplines that reward serious practice.

The second is continuing to invest in software engineering fundamentals. Data structures. System design. Debugging. Performance. Security. These matter more when AI writes the code, not less. You cannot specify what you do not understand. You cannot judge code you cannot read. You cannot maintain what you never learned.

Engineers who pull ahead invest in both. Fundamentals without Gen AI fluency leaves you slow. Gen AI fluency without fundamentals leaves you producing code you cannot judge or maintain.

Most of us got into this work because we wanted to build things — and watch someone use them. The code was a vehicle for that. Gen AI removes friction between us and the destination.

Seventy years from now, software engineering will mean what it has always meant: delivering software that works for the people who use it.

The tools will be unrecognizable. The role will be intact.

The engineers who hold the role will be the ones who keep learning — the new tools and the old fundamentals, side by side, again and again.
